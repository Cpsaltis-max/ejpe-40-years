European Journal of Psychology of Education, 1986-2026

A sociogenetic report on forty years of EJPE

This report synthesizes topic modelling, bilingual-normalized abstracts, full-text methodology coding, author geography, editorial-board composition, founding-project article coding, and citation-lineage analysis. The aim is to describe how the journal’s intellectual object changed over time: from a founding social constructivist and socio-institutional project of psychology of education toward a broader international field of context-sensitive educational psychology.

Core interpretation. EJPE began close to a founding social constructivist and socio-institutional project of psychology of education, articulated by Anne-Nelly Perret-Clermont, Michel Gilly and other early figures: interaction, development, didactic situations, social mediation, and institutional context were central. Over forty years, that founding orientation persisted but became one lineage within a larger international educational psychology ecology, especially motivation/self-regulation, achievement, psychometrics, and policy-oriented evidence.

The most compact empirical expression of this shift is the declining relative weight of fundamental citation lineages: in the citation-lineage analysis they account for 56.3% of article-author citation links in the 1980s, 42.6% in the 1990s, 26.3% in the 2000s, 10.9% in the 2010s, and 8.8% in the 2020s. The journal did not simply abandon the founding project; rather, the project was progressively surrounded by a wider, more standardized, international educational psychology.

1. Corpus and Data

The final corpus contains 1,885 EJPE articles from 1986 to 2026. Abstracts were enriched and normalized, including French-to-English normalization where needed, before the final BERTopic model was created with OpenAI large embeddings. Full-text files were used for methodology coding and reference extraction.

Why this matters for a sociogenetic history. The analysis does not treat EJPE as a static archive. It follows changes in the journal’s object of knowledge, methods, geography, editorial composition, and citational ancestry across successive historical periods.

4. Geography and Editorial Composition

The journal’s social base also changed. Authorship became more internationally distributed, and multi-country authorship rose from 9.6% in the 1980s to 27.6% in the 2020s. Editorial-board diversity also broadened: the cleaned board data show 20 represented countries in 1986 and 26 in 2022.

5. The Founding Project Question

The founding-project analysis asks whether an article treats education mainly as a site for applying general psychological constructs, or whether the educational situation itself is the object of explanation. Anne-Nelly Perret-Clermont and other founding figures define the broader social constructivist and socio-institutional project; Gilly’s writings are used here as explicit textual anchors for that project, alongside Doise, Mugny, Carugati, Pontecorvo, Vergnaud, Schubauer-Leoni and others. We coded IV/DV structure, the role of education in the explanation, and the resulting founding-project category.

Fundamental contextualized educational interaction.
Education is not only a setting. The didactic, institutional, dialogic, or interactional situation explains learning, knowledge construction, development, or subjectivity.
Context-sensitive educational psychology.
Educational context matters, but the explanation often remains centred on individual outcomes, constructs, or predictors.

Interpretation. The founding social constructivist and socio-institutional tradition remains visible through Piaget, Vygotsky, Doise, Mugny, Perret-Clermont, Carugati, Gilly, Bruner, Rogoff, Wertsch, Mercer and related figures. But the largest later citation ecology is motivation/self-regulation/identity, followed by didactics/classroom practices, psychometrics/statistics, developmental/ecological work, and individual-differences/cognitive-achievement work.

7. Sociogenetic Interpretation

The overall pattern supports a sociogenetic reading of EJPE itself. The journal began as an institutional attempt to construct a European psychology of education with a distinctive object: educational interaction, development, knowledge construction, and institutionally situated learning. That object was never erased. It survives in topics, citation lineages, and recurrent authors.

However, the journal’s later development also shows internationalization, methodological standardization, and assimilation into the broader field of educational psychology. Motivation, self-regulation, achievement, psychometrics, assessment, well-being, and policy evidence become increasingly central. The journal’s identity therefore appears hybrid: neither a pure continuation of the founding project nor a simple conversion into applied psychology.

Proposed formulation for the paper. EJPE can be interpreted as a sociogenetic object in its own right: a journal born from a European social constructivist and socio-institutional project, then progressively transformed through international authorship, methodological normalization, and the expansion of educational psychology as a global research field.

Possible Argument Structure

  • Origin: the journal’s early decades are anchored in Piagetian, post-Piagetian, sociocultural, social-representational, and didactic traditions.
  • Expansion: the article corpus broadens toward motivation, achievement, assessment, and individual/contextual predictors of educational outcomes.
  • Institutionalization: author geography and editorial-board diversity broaden, while methods become more standardized and quantitative.
  • Hybridization: the founding social constructivist and socio-institutional tradition persists as a minority but meaningful lineage within a broader international educational psychology.

8. Methods and Caveats

This report combines several layers of evidence. Abstract-based BERTopic provides the semantic map. Full-text sections support methodology coding. OpenAI large embeddings were used after bilingual normalization. The founding-project coding uses title/abstract-based inference of explanatory structure. Citation lineages use extracted reference lists from full-text files and a canonical-name cleaning layer.

  • Bilingualism: French abstracts were normalized before the final embedding analysis, reducing language artefacts in topic modelling.
  • Reference extraction: citation extraction from text files is strong enough for lineage analysis but not equivalent to a hand-verified bibliographic database.
  • Canonical names: safe variants such as A.-N. Perret-Clermont and L. S. Vygotsky were merged; common surnames were not automatically merged.
  • Founding-project coding: article categories are interpretive and should be reported as AI-assisted content coding with manual review, not as objective ground truth.
  • Editorial-board data: board composition is a complementary institutional layer and should not be treated as a direct causal explanation of article content.
